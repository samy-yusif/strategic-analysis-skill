# Strategy Frameworks Cheat Sheet

The scales, formulas and rules the skill applies. They follow the Fred David strategic-management sequence, with a few corrections to mistakes that show up often in practice.

## Sequence

1. **Input stage**: External (PESTEL → Porter → CPM → IPM → EFE) and Internal (7S → Value Discipline → VRIO → Value Chain → Financials → Ratios → IPM → IFE)
2. **Matching stage**: SWOT/TOWS → SPACE → IE → Grand Strategy → BCG / Entry options
3. **Decision stage**: QSPM
4. **Direction**: Vision, Mission, Values (for a new venture these come after the decision)
5. **Implementation**: Objectives → Generic strategy → Action plan → Budget → Projection → Risk register
6. **Evaluation**: Balanced Scorecard

---

## Vision: 7 criteria
Inspiring · Paints a clear picture · Desirable · Unique · Focused · Feasible · Easy to communicate

## Mission: 11 components
Customer · Products/Services · Markets · Survival · Growth · Profitability · Technology · Philosophy · Self-concept · Public image · Employees

---

## PESTEL
Political, Economic, Social (including Hofstede's 6 dimensions), Technological, Environmental, Legal. Every factor gets evidence (a number and year), a Type (Opportunity/Threat) and a source.

## Porter's Five Forces
New entrants · Supplier power · Buyer power · Substitutes · Rivalry. Rate each High/Moderate/Low with evidence, then give overall industry attractiveness.

## CPM: Competitive Profile Matrix
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Factors | 8–12 key success factors |
| Weight | 0.0–1.0, sum = 1.00, same for every firm |
| Rating | 1 major weakness · 2 minor weakness · 3 minor strength · 4 major strength |
| Score | Weight × Rating |

## IPM: Impact-Probability Matrix (factor filter)
Occurrence and Impact are coded **1 = High, 2 = Mid, 3 = Low**.

`Priority = (Occurrence − 1) × 3 + Impact` → 1 (most critical) … 9 (least)

| | Impact High | Mid | Low |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Occurrence High** | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| **Mid** | 4 | 5 | 6 |
| **Low** | 7 | 8 | 9 |

Factors with priority **1–4** move on to the EFE / IFE.

## EFE: External Factor Evaluation
- Weights sum to 1.00
- Rating 1–4 = how well the firm's strategy **responds** (applies to both opportunities and threats)
- Total weighted score: 2.50 = average

## McKinsey 7S
Hard: Strategy, Structure, Systems. Soft: Shared values, Style, Staff, Skills. Look for misalignment between them.

## Value Discipline Triad (Treacy & Wiersema)
Product Leadership · Customer Intimacy · Operational Excellence, each scored 0–100. Lead in one, stay at threshold in the other two.

## VRIO
| V | R | I | O | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ✗ | | | | Competitive disadvantage |
| ✓ | ✗ | | | Parity |
| ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | | Temporary advantage |
| ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | Unused advantage |
| ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | Sustained advantage |

## Porter Value Chain
Primary: Inbound logistics, Operations, Outbound logistics, Marketing & sales, Services.
Support: Firm infrastructure, Technology development, Procurement, HRM.

## Financial ratios
Profitability (gross, operating and net margin, ROA, ROE) · Liquidity (current, quick, cash) · Leverage (liabilities/assets, interest-bearing debt/equity, interest coverage) · Activity (turnovers, DIO, DSO, DPO, cash conversion cycle) · Growth (CAGR).
**Compare against competitors and the firm's own trend, not textbook norms.**

## IFE: Internal Factor Evaluation
- Weights sum to 1.00
- **Strengths: 3 (minor) or 4 (major). Weaknesses: 1 (major) or 2 (minor).** Rating a weakness 3–4 is a frequent error that inflates the score.
- 2.50 = average

## SWOT → TOWS
S/W/O/T lists = exactly the IFE and EFE factors.
| | Opportunities | Threats |
|---|---|---|
| **Strengths** | SO (Maxi-Maxi) | ST (Maxi-Mini) |
| **Weaknesses** | WO (Mini-Maxi) | WT (Mini-Mini) |

## SPACE Matrix
| Axis | Range |
|---|---|
| Financial Strength (FS) | +1 … +6 |
| Industry Strength (IS) | +1 … +6 |
| Environmental Stability (ES) | −1 (best) … −6 (worst) |
| Competitive Advantage (CA) | −1 (best) … −6 (worst) |

`X = avg CA + avg IS` · `Y = avg FS + avg ES`
Quadrants: (+,+) Aggressive · (−,+) Conservative · (−,−) Defensive · (+,−) Competitive

## IE Matrix
X = IFE total, Y = EFE total; bands 3.0–4.0 / 2.0–2.99 / 1.0–1.99.
- Cells I, II, IV → **Grow & Build**
- Cells III, V, VII → **Hold & Maintain**
- Cells VI, VIII, IX → **Harvest / Divest**

## Grand Strategy Matrix
| | Weak position | Strong position |
|---|---|---|
| **Rapid growth** | Q2: intensive strategies, horizontal integration, divestiture | Q1: market development and penetration, product development, integration, related diversification |
| **Slow growth** | Q3: retrenchment, diversification, divestiture | Q4: diversification, joint ventures |

## QSPM
- Same factors and weights as EFE + IFE
- AS: 1 not attractive · 2 somewhat · 3 reasonably · 4 highly attractive
- If a factor doesn't affect the choice, mark it "—" across the whole row
- TAS = Weight × AS; the highest total wins
- Sensitivity: does the winner change if the top 3 weights move ±5 points?

---

## Balanced Scorecard
Financial · Customer · Internal process · Learning & growth. Each KPI has a baseline, a target and an initiative linked to the action plan.

## Success levers (this skill's addition)
Every factor gets a **Solution** and an **Expected effect**: owner, cost level, time, and which score it moves (e.g., "CPM delivery time 2 → 3").
